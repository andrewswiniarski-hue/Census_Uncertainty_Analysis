# WORKLOG.md — Team Work Log

A running record of the work done on this project: **who did it, when, and what came of it.** Newest entries go at the top.

**Why we keep this:** with the whole team working in one repo, this file is how we stay aware of each other's progress without reading every commit. It is also part of our defensibility story — when we assemble the final report and present to Census mentors, this is the paper trail of what was done, by whom, and what was found along the way.

---

## How to add an entry

When you finish a piece of work — a script, an analysis, a notebook, a document, a meeting deliverable — copy the template below to the **top of the Log section** (newest first) and fill it in. One entry per completed chunk of work; tiny fixes (typos, formatting) don't need one.

```markdown
### YYYY-MM-DD — Your Name — Short title of the work
- **Area:** ingestion | analysis | notebooks | docs | dashboard | project management
- **What was done:** 2–4 plain-English sentences a teammate on another workstream can follow.
- **Findings / decisions:** anything learned, decided, or surprising — data quirks especially. ("None" is fine.)
- **Files:** paths, notebook names, and/or commit hashes.
```

Ground rules:

- **Plain English first.** Write the summary so a teammate who wasn't there understands it without opening the code.
- **Surprises go here.** A weird distribution, an impossible value, a data quirk — log it even if it feels minor. (Also raise it with the team; see the "Data landmines" list in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).)
- **Update the log in the same commit as the work** whenever possible, so the log and the repo never drift apart.
- New statistical terms still get defined in [docs/glossary.md](docs/glossary.md) — the log links to work, the glossary explains terms.

### 2026-07-28 — Garrett Spangler — Financial EDA: income imputation is a separate, uneven, and largely invisible error source

- **Area:** ingestion / analysis / docs
- **What was done:** Built the financial-data exploration end to end at vintages 2024 and 2023. `verify_api_surfaces.py` confirms every table ID, variable name, bracket bound and geography level against live Census metadata before anything downstream uses it. Six pull scripts in `ingestion/` follow existing convention (four-part docstring, `.env` handling, `GEO_LEVELS` kwargs, sanity report, parquet to `data/raw/`), each with `--dry-run`. Four analysis modules with 98 passing tests: `common.py` (vintage, sentinel cleaning, MOE→CV delegating to `acs.cv` so the convention cannot drift), `alloc_denominator.py`, `alloc_profile.py`, `replicate.py` (MOEs from the 80 PUMS replicate weights). Two runners regenerate the findings from data on disk. Sources: ACS tract allocation flags (B99), NJ PUMS person records (376,037 age 15+), SAIPE counties.
- **Findings / decisions:** Income is imputed ~50× more often than any demographic item — median 39.3% across 2,181 NJ tracts against 0.8% age, 0.7% race, 0.0% sex — and is statistically independent of them (Spearman 0.03–0.18), so it is not a proxy for hard-to-survey areas. Underneath: 98.2% of public-assistance allocations impute a *zero* rather than an amount, against 28.5% for wages; 44.3% of flagged records carry all six flags (whole-person substitution), and removing them widens the spread across income sources from 1.45× to 4.30×; wholly-substituted share falls monotonically 18.4%→9.4% across education and 19.2%→9.4% by age, sex is a null at 1.08×, and replicate weights make every adjacent pair distinguishable at 90%. `B98031`/`B98032`, the only published measure of how much of a product was inferred, return 21/21 at county and 0/2,181 at tract. **Decision:** never quote a bare allocation rate — under eight defensible denominators wages rank anywhere from 1st to 6th. Three more landmines: (1) PUMS income N/A codes are not uniform — `WAGP`/`SSP`/`RETP`/`PAP` use −1 but `SEMP`/`INTP` use −10001, so a blanket `< 0` filter corrupts the two sources of most interest; (2) `FHINCP` is a household flag arriving on person rows — dedupe on `SERIALNO` or NJ reads 9.07M households instead of 3.4M; (3) `ingestion/pull_acs_alloc_nj.py` passes its `B98031` range check vacuously at tract, where the column is 100% null and `.all()` on an empty series returns `True`.
- **Files:** `verify_api_surfaces.py`, `report_alloc_denominators.py`, `report_alloc_profile.py`; `analysis/{common,alloc_denominator,alloc_profile,replicate}.py`; six `ingestion/pull_*.py`; `tests/` (98 tests); `requirements-dev.txt`; `notebooks/08-allocation-denominator-sensitivity.ipynb`, `notebooks/09-who-gets-inferred-nj-pums.ipynb`; outputs `data/raw/` + `data/processed/` (local only, regenerable); method and full sweeps in [docs/api-surface-verified.md](docs/api-surface-verified.md), [docs/api-surface-b99-income.md](docs/api-surface-b99-income.md), [docs/allocation-denominator-sensitivity.md](docs/allocation-denominator-sensitivity.md), [docs/allocation-profile-nj.md](docs/allocation-profile-nj.md)
